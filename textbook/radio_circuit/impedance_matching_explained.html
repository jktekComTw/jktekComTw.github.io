Impedance Matching Explained

Why $\Gamma_L=0$ means matched, what happens when it isn't, and how it affects power transfer.

1. The Concept

A transmission line has characteristic impedance $Z_0$. When the load impedance $Z_L$ equals $Z_0$, all power is absorbed — no reflection. When $Z_L \neq Z_0$, part of the wave bounces back.

The reflection coefficient quantifies the mismatch:

$$\Gamma_L = \frac{Z_L - Z_0}{Z_L + Z_0}$$

$\Gamma_L = 0$ → perfect match  |  $|\Gamma_L| = 1$ → total reflection

3. What This Means for Noise

In the lossy-line noise figure context from the study guide:

  • Load matched ($\Gamma_L = 0$): noise traveling toward the load is fully absorbed — good.
  • Source mismatched ($\Gamma_s \neq 0$): noise from the line that travels back toward the source gets reflected again into the line, adding extra noise power. This increases the noise figure beyond what the line loss alone would predict.

The noise figure of a mismatched lossy line:

$$F = L \cdot \frac{1 - |\Gamma_s|^2 \cdot |S_{11}|^2}{1 - |\Gamma_s|^2} \cdot \left(1 + \frac{(L-1)(1-|\Gamma_s|^2|S_{11}|^2)}{1-|\Gamma_s|^2}\right)$$

When $\Gamma_s = 0$, this simplifies to $F = L$ (loss-only).
