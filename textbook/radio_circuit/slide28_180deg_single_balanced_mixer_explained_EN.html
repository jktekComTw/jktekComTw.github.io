The mirror image of the 90° version: LO isolation is unconditionally perfect, the RF match depends on the diodes

Annotated reconstruction of slide 28 — the hybrid is now drawn as a rat-race ring: three $\lambda/4$ short arms (blue) + one $3\lambda/4$ long arm (red). RF enters the Σ port (port 1), LO enters the Δ port (port 4). Phase labels show LO behavior at port 2/3 (anti-phase, $\pm 90°$); RF reaches both diodes in-phase at $-90°$.

What every element in the figure means

① The two input ports: RF input (Σ port) and LO input (Δ port)

The two arrows on the left inject, respectively:

  • RF input(blue, entering port 1 = Σ port, the sum port): the radio-frequency signal to be downconverted, $v_{RF}(t)=V_{RF}\cos\omega_{RF}t$. Σ denotesin-phase splitting — the RF is divided equally between the two diodes with the same phase.
  • LO input(red, entering port 4 = Δ port, the difference port): the local-oscillator signal $v_{LO}(t)=V_{LO}\cos\omega_{LO}t$, which isresponsible for switching the diodes. Δ denotesanti-phase splitting — the LO reaches the two diodes in opposite phase (a $180°$ difference).
The fundamental difference between the 90° and 180° topologies
90° hybrid: the RF/LO phase differences of the two paths areswapped with each other(asymmetric).
180° hybrid:RF in phase, LO in anti-phase — this Σ/Δ structure is the hallmark of the 180° topology. As a result, the RF reflections at thetwodiodes are identical ($V_\Gamma^1=V_\Gamma^2$), so travelling back to the Δ port (LO) they cancel exactly, while travelling back to the Σ port (RF) they add exactly.
Terminology: what exactly is a “hybrid”?

Hybrid(hybrid junction / hybrid coupler) is the microwave-engineering name for a particular class offour-port passive component. It splits one signalinto two, or combines two signalsinto one, while strictly controlling thephase difference and power ratioof the image-reject mixer at low IF.

The four defining properties

  1. Four ports (4-port), each of which, taken on its own, isperfectly matched($S_{ii}=0$).
  2. Equal power split(3 dB): a signal incident on any port → each of the two output ports takes half the power.
  3. There is afixed phase differencebetween the two output ports ($180°$ for this topology).
  4. The remaining port iscompletely isolatedof the image-reject mixer at low IF.

Concrete implementations of the 180° hybrid

  • Rat-race ring(figure above): a ring of transmission line with a total circumference of $3\lambda/2$; of the four ports, three adjacent pairs are $\lambda/4$ apart (the short arms) and the remaining pair is $3\lambda/4$ apart (the long arm). By far the most common implementation.
  • Magic-T: a waveguide implementation; the same Σ/Δ structure, but working at mm-wave.
  • Transformer balun: a 180° balun built from magnetic components at low frequencies ($< 1$ GHz).

180° vs. 90°: the 90° version is used where quadrature (I/Q) outputs are needed (image-reject mixers, quadrature splitters); the 180° version is used where differential drive is needed — anti-parallel diode-pair mixers, balanced amplifiers, Σ/Δ receivers.

② The central block: a 3 dB 180° hybrid (rat-race ring implementation)

What is drawn inside the block is a rat-race (ring) coupler: a ring of transmission line with a total circumference of $3\lambda/2$.

  • Three $\lambda/4$ short arms (blue): connecting ports 1–2, 2–3 and 3–4 respectively.
  • One $3\lambda/4$ long arm (red): taking the long way round from port 4 back to port 1.
  • All four ports have a characteristic impedance of $\sqrt{2}Z_0\approx 70.7\,\Omega$ (in a 50 Ω system), connected to the external 50 Ω through a $\lambda/4$ impedance transformation.

The $\lambda/2$ path-length differencebetween $\lambda/4$ and $3\lambda/4$ isthe very reason the whole hybrid can produce a $180°$ phase difference(every extra $\lambda/2$ travelled adds $180°$).

The scattering matrix of the complete 4-port structure (port numbering as in ①②③④) is:

$$[S] = \frac{-j}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 0 & \cssId{rfd1a}{1} & \cssId{rfd2a}{1} & 0 \\ \cssId{rfd1b}{1} & 0 & 0 & \cssId{lod1a}{-1} \\ \cssId{rfd2b}{1} & 0 & 0 & \cssId{lod2a}{1} \\ 0 & \cssId{lod1b}{-1} & \cssId{lod2b}{1} & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
Reading the [S]-matrix elements (port 1 = RF/Σ, 2 = diode 1, 3 = diode 2, 4 = LO/Δ)

How to read it: $S_{ij}=$ the complex voltage ratio for a wave incident at port $j$ and emerging at port $i$. Every non-zero element hasmagnitude $1/\sqrt{2}$(an equal 3 dB split) plus an overall $-j$ prefix; the $\pm 1$ in the matrix sets the additional $0°$ or $180°$ of phase.

Element Value Path ($j\to i$) Physical meaning
$S_{11},S_{22},S_{33},S_{44}$ $0$ self → self All four ports areperfectly matched(return loss = ∞)
$S_{14}=S_{41}$ $0$ RF(Σ) ↔ LO(Δ) The Σ and Δ ports areinherently isolated(not by virtue of symmetry)
$S_{23}=S_{32}$ $0$ diode 1 ↔ diode 2 The two diode ports are isolated from each other

$S_{31}=S_{13}$ $\dfrac{-j}{\sqrt{2}}$ RF (1) → diode 2 (3) Σ in-phase split: half power,$-90°$(in phasewith the cell above!)
$S_{24}=S_{42}$ $\dfrac{+j}{\sqrt{2}}$ LO (4) → diode 1 (2) Δ anti-phase split: half power,$+90°$
$S_{34}=S_{43}$ $\dfrac{-j}{\sqrt{2}}$ LO (4) → diode 2 (3) Δ anti-phase split: half power,$-90°$(in anti-phasewith the cell above!)

The core symmetry: $S_{21}=S_{31}$ (RF reaches both diodes in phase) but $S_{24}=-S_{34}$ (the LO reaches the two diodes in anti-phase). This is the mathematical definition of Σ/Δ behaviour.

Unitarity and reciprocity: the matrix is symmetric (reciprocal), $[S]^\dagger[S]=[I]$ (lossless), and the diagonal is all zeros (matched). Together these three properties pin it down as an ideal 180° 4-port hybrid.

③ The “$-90°$” and “$+90°$” marked beside port 2 / port 3

The angles in the figure are the phases of the LO signalas it arrives at ports 2 and 3 after entering at port 4 (to a suitable reference):

  • LO (4) → diode 1 (port 2): via $S_{24}=+j/\sqrt{2}$ → phase $\mathbf{+90°}$
  • LO (4) → diode 2 (port 3): via $S_{34}=-j/\sqrt{2}$ → phase $\mathbf{-90°}$

The LO phases at the two ports differ by $180°$ — that is where the name “180° hybrid” comes from, and it is the anti-phase split of the Δ port.

⚠ Note: the “$\pm 90°$” in the figure isnotthe phase of the RF. Once the RF enters from the Σ port it reaches both diodes withthe same $-90°$($S_{21}=S_{31}=-j/\sqrt{2}$) — that is, the RF is common-mode and the LO is differential-mode. Comparing them explicitly:

④ The two diodes (an anti-parallel pair)

Exactly as in the 90° version — the two diodes are connected in series withopposite orientation, so that the upper one has an effective coefficient of $+a_1$ and the lower one $-a_1$.

Why does the signal-flow diagram say “diode pair, ±a₁v²”?

① Where does $a_1 v^2$ come from? The I–V curve of a single diode is nonlinear; expanding the current as a Taylor series in the applied voltage:

$$i(v) = a_0 v + a_1 v^2 + a_2 v^3 + \cdots$$

The numbering convention in this course (and in Pozar) calls thesecond-order term$a_1$. So $a_1$ is thesquare-law coefficient, and it isthe engine of the entire mixer — without the $v^2$ term there is no mixing.

② Where does the “$\pm$” come from? The two diodes are connected in series back to back, so the lower one carries aminus signat some port:

$$i_1(t) \approx \mathbf{+}a_1\bigl[v_1(t)+v_n(t)\bigr]^2, \qquad i_2(t) \approx \mathbf{-}a_1\bigl[v_2(t)+v_n(t)\bigr]^2$$

$v_n(t)$ = the AM noise on the LO; because the hybrid is linear, both diodes see the same $v_n(t)$ (common mode).

③ Summing:

$$i_o = i_1 + i_2 \approx a_1\bigl[v_1^2 - v_2^2\bigr] + 2a_1 v_n(t)\bigl[v_1 - v_2\bigr]$$

$v_1^2 - v_2^2$ cancels the common-mode terms and keeps only the RF×LO difference-frequency product.

⑤ The block on the right (summing junction) + ⑥ Low-pass filter

$i_1$ and $i_2$ add at a common node → the whole lot is low-pass filtered, leaving the IF tone. For the 180° version, $v_1$ and $v_2$ relate to the RF/LO as:

$$v_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl[V_{RF}\sin\omega_{RF}t - V_{LO}\sin\omega_{LO}t\bigr], \quad v_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl[V_{RF}\sin\omega_{RF}t + V_{LO}\sin\omega_{LO}t\bigr]$$

Note that the RF parts of $v_1$ and $v_2$ havethe same sign(common mode) while the LO parts haveopposite signs(differential mode). Substituting into $v_1^2-v_2^2$ gives

$$v_1^2 - v_2^2 = -2 V_{RF} V_{LO} \sin\omega_{RF}t \sin\omega_{LO}t = -V_{RF}V_{LO}\bigl[\cos(\omega_{RF}-\omega_{LO})t - \cos(\omega_{RF}+\omega_{LO})t\bigr]$$

After the LPF, the IF output is:

$$\color{#c01020}{\boldsymbol{i_{IF}(t) \approx -a_1 V_{RF}V_{LO}\cos(\omega_{RF}-\omega_{LO})t = -\tfrac{1}{2}g'_{DC}V_{RF}V_{LO}\cos\omega_{IF}t}}$$

Note: the IF of the 180° version has acosform (versus sin for the 90° version); the amplitudes are identical and only the $90°$ of phase differs.

⑦ The red curled-back arrows: $V_\Gamma^{RF}, V_\Gamma^{LO}, V_\Gamma^1, V_\Gamma^2$

Where do $V_\Gamma^1, V_\Gamma^2$ come from? (180° version: both diodes see the same RF voltage)

Step 1 — Σ sends the RF to both diodes in phase. From the $[S]$ matrix:

$$V_1 = S_{21}V_{RF} = \frac{-j}{\sqrt{2}}V_{RF}, \qquad V_2 = S_{31}V_{RF} = \frac{-j}{\sqrt{2}}V_{RF}$$

Note that $V_1 = V_2$! This is the crux of the Σ in-phase split — both diodes see exactly the same RF voltage phasor. (Contrast the 90° version: $V_1\propto -j$ and $V_2\propto -1$ differ by $90°$ and are not equal.)

Step 2 — the mismatched diodes reflect $\Gamma$ times as much back.

$$V_\Gamma^1 = \Gamma\,V_1 = \frac{-j\Gamma V_{RF}}{\sqrt{2}}, \qquad V_\Gamma^2 = \Gamma\,V_2 = \frac{-j\Gamma V_{RF}}{\sqrt{2}}$$

$V_\Gamma^1 = V_\Gamma^2$ (same amplitude, same phase).

Step 3 — the hybrid redistributes these twoidenticalreflections to port 1 (Σ/RF) and port 4 (Δ/LO):

$$V_\Gamma^{RF} = S_{12}V_\Gamma^1 + S_{13}V_\Gamma^2, \qquad V_\Gamma^{LO} = S_{42}V_\Gamma^1 + S_{43}V_\Gamma^2$$
The key result (the main point)
Substituting:
$$\begin{aligned} V_\Gamma^{RF} &= \frac{-j}{\sqrt{2}}\!\cdot\!\frac{-j\Gamma V_{RF}}{\sqrt{2}} + \frac{-j}{\sqrt{2}}\!\cdot\!\frac{-j\Gamma V_{RF}}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{-\Gamma V_{RF}}{2} + \frac{-\Gamma V_{RF}}{2} = -\Gamma V_{RF} \\[4pt] \color{#c01020}{\boldsymbol{V_\Gamma^{LO}}} &\color{#c01020}{\boldsymbol{= \frac{+j}{\sqrt{2}}\!\cdot\!\frac{-j\Gamma V_{RF}}{\sqrt{2}} + \frac{-j}{\sqrt{2}}\!\cdot\!\frac{-j\Gamma V_{RF}}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\Gamma V_{RF}}{2} + \frac{-\Gamma V_{RF}}{2} = 0}} \quad\color{#c01020}{\boldsymbol{\text{(the reflection coefficient at the LO port is 0)}}} \end{aligned}$$
Even if the two diodes are completely mismatched,the LO port always looks perfectly isolated ($V_\Gamma^{LO}=0$) — the two paths of the Δ split are $180°$ apart and cancel. All of the reflection goes to the RF port instead (adding in phase), giving $|V_\Gamma^{RF}/V_{RF}|=|\Gamma|$. So what this 180° hybrid buys you is “RF-to-LO isolation is always perfect”, at the cost of “the RF match depends on how well the diodes are matched”.
Interpreting $-\Gamma V_{RF}$ and $|V_\Gamma^{RF}/V_{RF}|=|\Gamma|$

① $-\Gamma V_{RF}$ is the value of $V_\Gamma^{RF}$ — the voltage that “leaks” back to the RF input port as reflection. Broken down: amplitude $= |\Gamma|\!\cdot\!|V_{RF}|$, phase inverted ($180°$).

② $|V_\Gamma^{RF}/V_{RF}|=|\Gamma|$ is the magnitude of the reflection coefficient at the RF port.

Overall conclusion (180° version): the LO port isunconditionally and perfectly isolated($V_\Gamma^{LO}=0$) ✅; the RF matchdepends on how well the diodes are matched($|V_\Gamma^{RF}/V_{RF}|=|\Gamma|$) ⚠. This is precisely the 90° version'smirror imageof the image-reject mixer at low IF.

Signal-flow overview (the whole thing in one equation)

$$\underbrace{V_{RF}\cos\omega_{RF}t}_{\text{port 1 = Σ}} + \underbrace{V_{LO}\cos\omega_{LO}t}_{\text{port 4 = Δ}} \xrightarrow{\text{[S]}_{180°}} \begin{cases} v_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[V_{RF}\sin\omega_{RF}t - V_{LO}\sin\omega_{LO}t] \\ v_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[V_{RF}\sin\omega_{RF}t + V_{LO}\sin\omega_{LO}t] \end{cases} \xrightarrow{\substack{\text{diode pair}\\ \pm a_1 v^2}} i_o(t) \xrightarrow{\text{LPF}} i_{IF}(t)$$
Step-by-step derivation: the complete algebra of the three transitions from the LHS (RF + LO inputs) to the RHS ($i_{IF}(t)$)

Transition 1: $[S]_{180°}$ matrix → $v_1, v_2$

The RF is incident at port 1 (Σ) and the LO at port 4 (Δ). Applying the hybrid's scattering matrix at ports 2 and 3:

$$V_2 = S_{21}V_{RF} + S_{24}V_{LO} = \tfrac{-j}{\sqrt{2}}V_{RF} + \tfrac{+j}{\sqrt{2}}V_{LO}$$ $$V_3 = S_{31}V_{RF} + S_{34}V_{LO} = \tfrac{-j}{\sqrt{2}}V_{RF} + \tfrac{-j}{\sqrt{2}}V_{LO}$$

Phasors back to the time domain: Multiplying by $-j$ ⇒ $\cos\omega t \to \sin\omega t$; multiplying by $+j$ ⇒ $\cos\omega t \to -\sin\omega t$.

$$v_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl[V_{RF}\sin\omega_{RF}t - V_{LO}\sin\omega_{LO}t\bigr], \quad v_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl[V_{RF}\sin\omega_{RF}t + V_{LO}\sin\omega_{LO}t\bigr]$$

Note that the RF parts of $v_1$ and $v_2$ have the same sign (Σ, common mode) while the LO parts have opposite signs (Δ, differential mode). This is the biggest difference from the 90° version.

Transition 2: the anti-parallel diode pair ($\pm a_1 v^2$) → $i_o(t)$

$i_o = a_1[v_1^2 - v_2^2] + 2a_1 v_n(t)[v_1 - v_2]$. Expanding $v_1^2$ and $v_2^2$:

$$v_1^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[V_{RF}^2\sin^2\omega_{RF}t - 2V_{RF}V_{LO}\sin\omega_{RF}t\sin\omega_{LO}t + V_{LO}^2\sin^2\omega_{LO}t\bigr]$$ $$v_2^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[V_{RF}^2\sin^2\omega_{RF}t + 2V_{RF}V_{LO}\sin\omega_{RF}t\sin\omega_{LO}t + V_{LO}^2\sin^2\omega_{LO}t\bigr]$$

Subtracting:The $V_{RF}^2$ and $V_{LO}^2$ terms cancel exactly(because both sides have the same sign)! What remains is:

$$v_1^2 - v_2^2 = -2 V_{RF}V_{LO}\sin\omega_{RF}t\sin\omega_{LO}t$$

Applying the product-to-sum identity $\sin A\sin B = \tfrac{1}{2}[\cos(A-B)-\cos(A+B)]$:

$$v_1^2 - v_2^2 = -V_{RF}V_{LO}\bigl[\cos(\omega_{RF}-\omega_{LO})t - \cos(\omega_{RF}+\omega_{LO})t\bigr]$$

$v_1 - v_2$ (needed for the AM-noise term):

$$v_1 - v_2 = -\sqrt{2}\,V_{LO}\sin\omega_{LO}t$$

The RF part cancels automatically (both sides have the same sign), leaving only the LO fundamental — this is how the 180° topology suppresses RF leakage into the IF!

Combining:

$$i_o(t) \approx -a_1 V_{RF}V_{LO}\bigl[\cos(\omega_{RF}-\omega_{LO})t - \cos(\omega_{RF}+\omega_{LO})t\bigr] - 2\sqrt{2}\,a_1 v_n(t) V_{LO}\sin\omega_{LO}t$$

Transition 3: LPF → $i_{IF}(t)$

The low-pass filter blocks the sum frequency $\omega_{RF}+\omega_{LO}$, the AM noise and the LO-fundamental product (all near the RF/LO band), leaving:

$$\color{#c01020}{\boldsymbol{i_{IF}(t) \approx -a_1 V_{RF}V_{LO}\cos(\omega_{RF}-\omega_{LO})t = -\tfrac{1}{2}g'_{DC}V_{RF}V_{LO}\cos\omega_{IF}t}}$$

Three sentences to take away

  1. The 180° hybrid is a Σ/Δ structure — the RF reaches both diodes in phase (common mode) and the LO reaches them in anti-phase (differential mode). Pozar implements it as a rat-race ring: three $\lambda/4$ arms plus one $3\lambda/4$ arm.
  2. It is the mirror image of the 90° version: the 90° hybrid kicks all the reflection out to the LO port (preserving the RF match); the 180° hybrid kicks it all out to the RF port (preserving LO isolation). The two have symmetric mathematics but complementary strengths.
  3. The IF output has a cos form(the 90° version has sin), and in both cases the amplitude is $a_1 V_{RF}V_{LO}$. The mechanism by which the anti-parallel pair suppresses AM noise through $v_1^2-v_2^2$ still applies.
